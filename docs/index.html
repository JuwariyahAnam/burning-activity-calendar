Two satellites. Two different counts.

Fire-days per year in the region: the number of 10 km grid cells with at least one detection, added up over every day.

    Line chart of fire-days per year from MODIS, VIIRS and the harmonized record.

    MODIS, since 2001

    MODIS on the Terra and Aqua satellites sees the ground in 1 km pixels. Its record is long, and it is the only one before 2012.

    VIIRS sees more

    From 2012, VIIRS looks in 375 m pixels, about seven times smaller in area. It catches small fires MODIS misses, so its counts are far higher. Joining the two raw records would invent a jump that never happened on the ground.

    One consistent record

    In the years both instruments flew, we learn how their counts relate, month by month. Applied to 2001–2011, it turns MODIS into VIIRS-equivalent fire-days, so the record reads as one line.

      How to read this page

      1. Choose a place. Pick a study area at the top of the page, click the district box to pick from the list (or type a name), or click a district on the map.
      2. Read the status. The coloured card says in one line whether burning there is normal, above usual or far above usual right now.
      3. Check the calendar. Each square is a month; brighter means more burning. Rows are years, so a bright column is the burning season.
      4. Ask a question. Under the status card, pick a question or type one, such as "how is Panna this week?". Answers come straight from the data.
      5. Pick any dates. Under the map, choose a from and to date to see just the fires of those days, compared with the same dates in other years.
      6. See the whole country. "When India burns", near the top, shows when every study area usually burns and which one starts next.
      7. Go deeper if you need to. See which districts burn most, what the next two weeks usually bring, compare two places, or download the data. Switch to Detailed for the method and statistics.

          Can you trust the join?

          We compare the three years after VIIRS began with the three years before. MODIS watched both periods with the same instrument, so its change is the real one. A good harmonization should match it.

          Tested on years it never saw

          For each year both instruments flew, we calibrate on the other years, predict that year's VIIRS fire-days from MODIS, and measure the error.

          Scaling factors in use

          VIIRS fire-days per MODIS fire-day, by calendar month, for the selected area.

          How the record is built

          1. Clean MODIS confidence 30 or more, VIIRS nominal or high, vegetation fires only, times converted to local dates.

          2. Count fire-days A 10 km cell counts once per day if any detection falls in it, removing duplicates and much of the pixel-size gap.

          3. One VIIRS satellite at a time Suomi NPP while it flew, then NOAA-20 scaled to match it, so new satellites in 2018 and 2023 add no false jumps.

          4. Calibrate Learn VIIRS ≈ k × MODIS from the overlap years, either one k or one per calendar month.

          5. Join and test Scale the MODIS-only years, then check the join on held-out years and across the 2012 switch.

          What the record can't tell you

          Fewer detections isn't less burning

          These satellites pass in the early afternoon and at night. Fires lit after the afternoon pass can be missed, and farmers in Punjab have shifted burning later in the day. A drop in counts can mean a change in timing.

          Recent months can change

          The latest months use near-real-time data. NASA replaces it with science-quality data about five months later, which can shift recent values slightly.

          An activity index, not burned area

          Fire-days count grid cells with at least one detection per day. They track how much and how often an area burns, not how many fires there were or how large.

          A flag is a prompt, not a warning

          A district is flagged by a simple rule against its own past: above the 90th percentile and 1.5 times the median for those dates. Check FIRMS and local reports before acting on it.

          What the columns mean
          harmonized, harmonized_fire_days
          Fire-days on the VIIRS scale: VIIRS where it exists, MODIS times the scaling factor before that.
          modis_raw, viirs_raw
          Fire-days counted by each instrument before scaling (empty where it did not fly).
          source
          Which record a day comes from: VIIRS observed, MODIS scaled, or no observation.
          fire-day
          One 0.1° grid cell (about 10 km) with at least one detection on one local day (UTC+5:30).
          usual, p10, p90
          Median and 10th/90th percentiles of the same dates in earlier complete years.
          start_10, halfway_50, end_90
          Dates by which 10%, 50% and 90% of that season's fire-days had happened.
          trend_pct_per_year, trend_p
          Theil–Sen slope of yearly fire-days as % of the median year, and the Mann–Kendall p-value (below 0.05: a clear trend).
