    MODIS, since 2001

    MODIS on the Terra and Aqua satellites sees the ground in 1 km pixels. Its record is long, and it is the only one before 2012.

    VIIRS sees more

    From 2012, VIIRS looks in 375 m pixels, about seven times smaller in area. It catches small fires MODIS misses, so its counts are far higher. Joining the two raw records would invent a jump that never happened on the ground.

    One consistent record

    In the years both instruments flew, we learn how their counts relate, month by month. Applied to 2001–2011, it turns MODIS into VIIRS-equivalent fire-days, so the record reads as one line.

    Can you trust the join?

    We compare the three years after VIIRS began with the three years before. MODIS watched both periods with the same instrument, so its change is the real one. A good harmonization should match it.

    Tested on years it never saw

    For each year both instruments flew, we calibrate on the other years, predict that year's VIIRS fire-days from MODIS, and measure the error.

    How the record is built

    1. Clean. MODIS confidence 30 or more, VIIRS nominal or high, vegetation fires only, times converted to local dates.

    2. Count fire-days. A 10 km cell counts once per day if any detection falls in it, removing duplicates and much of the pixel-size gap.

    3. One VIIRS satellite at a time. S-NPP while it flew, NOAA-20 after, so new satellites in 2018 and 2023 add no false jumps.

    4. Calibrate. Learn VIIRS ≈ k × MODIS from the overlap years, either one k or one per calendar month.

    5. Join and test. Scale the MODIS-only years, then check the join on held-out years and across the 2012 switch.

    What the record can't tell you

    Fewer detections isn't less burning

    These satellites pass in the early afternoon and at night. Fires lit after the afternoon pass can be missed, and farmers in Punjab have shifted burning later in the day. A drop in counts can mean a change in timing.

    Recent months can change

    The latest months use near-real-time data. NASA replaces it with science-quality data about five months later, which can shift recent values slightly.

    An activity index, not burned area

    Fire-days count grid cells with at least one detection per day. They track how much and how often an area burns, not how many fires there were or how large.
